The handshake circle

n people stand in a circle and every pair shakes hands once. Each person shakes n − 1 hands, but that counts every handshake twice — once from each end. In triangles mode the same idea counts groups of three, which overcounts by 3 × 2 × 1 = 6.

Five traps

Read before any test

Not deciding whether order matters

Choosing 2 reps from 8 students is 28. Choosing a captain and a vice-captain from 8 is 56. The difference is whether swapping the two people gives a new answer. Decide that before you write a single number.

Letting a number start with 0

There are 900 three-digit numbers, not 1000: the first digit has 9 choices (1–9), the others 10. Every digit question needs the first digit handled on its own, before the rest.

Counting 'at least one' by cases, and double-counting

3-digit numbers with at least one 7: counting 'a 7 first' + 'a 7 second' + 'a 7 third' counts 777 three times. Count the opposite instead: 900 − (numbers with no 7 at all) = 900 − 648 = 252.

Arranging repeated letters as if they were different

LEVEL has 5 letters, but the two Ls and two Es are interchangeable, so 5! = 120 counts each arrangement 2 × 2 = 4 times. The answer is 30. Divide by the factorial of each repeat count.

Adding when you should multiply

A starter and a main and a dessert: multiply (3 × 4 × 2 = 24). A starter or a dessert: add (3 + 2 = 5). If your answer to a 'choose one of each' question is small, you probably added.
