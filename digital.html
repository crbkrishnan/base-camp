Our Digital World

Two ideas run through this whole page. First: almost everything you do online leaves a copy somewhere you cannot reach, so the useful question is never can I delete it but should I post it. Second: online, nobody can see who is lying and who is guessing, so you have to check — sources, licences, identities, passwords. Everything else is detail hanging off those two.

Five traps

Read before any test

“If I delete it, it's gone.”

Wrong: deleting removes your copy. Anyone who took a screenshot, saved it or shared it still has theirs, and a lot of your footprint (browsing history, location, what other people post about you) was never yours to delete. Write: deleting removes my copy, but copies others made, and data collected passively, can remain.

“It's fine, I credited the photographer.”

Crediting is manners, not permission. The creator owns the copyright automatically; you need their permission or a licence that allows your use, and some licences allow credited reuse while others forbid it. Write: I need the owner's permission or a licence that allows this use, and I must follow its conditions, such as giving credit.

“Everybody's forwarding it, so it must be true.”

Forwards measure how shareable a message is, not whether anyone checked it. Misinformation spreads because the people passing it on believe it. Write: I would check the source, the date, the evidence and whether reliable independent outlets report it. Many forwards are not on that list.

Mixing up the four cyberbullying words

“Trolling is when someone pretends to be someone else” is wrong: that is catfishing. Trolling is deliberately provocative posting to upset people. Fraping is using someone's logged-in account without permission; dissing is spreading hurtful material about someone. Four words, four different acts: say what the person does.

“A firewall protects my computer from viruses.”

Wrong job. A firewall filters network traffic against rules; antivirus scans files and programs for malware; anti-spyware hunts programs that secretly record and send your data. Write the verb that belongs to each: block, scan, detect and remove.
