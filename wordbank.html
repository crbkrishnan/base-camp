Words and Spelling

शब्द और वर्तनी

English never asks whether a table is masculine. Hindi asks every time, and then makes the adjective, the verb and का / की / के follow the answer. Get the gender wrong and three words are wrong, not one. The other half of the page is spelling: one small mark can turn दिन (day) into दीन (poor) and no spell-checker will save you. Learn 178 nouns with their genders and plurals, 68 adjectives, the time words, and the small marks that decide whether a word is right.

The spelling guide

the marks · 27 confusable pairs

Hindi is spelled the way it sounds, so nearly every spelling mistake is a sound you did not listen to: short or long, flat or wide, nasal or not. Say the word out loud slowly, one sound at a time, before you write the mark.

A trap the eye plays on you. The short ि is written to the left of its letter but pronounced after it: कि is “ki”, not “ik”. The long ी sits on the right, where you would expect it. If you can see which side the stroke is on, you can see which vowel it is.

ँ · chandrabinduThe moon-and-dot. A nasal vowel, and it is used when nothing else sits above the line: आँख, साँस, कहाँ, माँ, हूँ, चाँद. Leave it out and you can change the word: साँस (breath) → सास (mother-in-law).
ं · anusvaraThe plain dot. Used before a consonant (पंखा, हंस, आनंद, गोंद) and whenever another mark already stands on top of the letter (में, नहीं, हैं, गेंद) — there is simply no room for a moon.
़ · nuktaA dot under a letter. It makes ज़ (z as in zoo), फ़ (f as in fan, with the teeth), and the flaps ड़ ढ़, which never start a word: बड़ा, पेड़, पढ़ना, सड़क. Urdu-origin words need it (ज़रा, बाज़ार, सज़ा); native words do not (फूल, जल). Over-dotting is a mistake too.
ः · visargaTwo dots, stacked. An ‘h’ echo at the end of Sanskrit-origin words: अतः (therefore), पुनः (again), प्रातः (morning), संभवतः (probably).
् · halantEvery plain consonant carries a hidden ‘a’: क is “ka”. The halant kills it: क् is just “k”. That is how two consonants join into one shape, and how a word can end on a bare consonant: अर्थात्.

र is the odd one. After a letter it becomes a tail (प्र, क्र, ट्र: प्रश्न, क्रम, राष्ट्र). Before a letter it becomes a hook on top of the next letter (धर्म, कर्म, सर्दी, पूर्ण) — you say the र first, but you write it last.

Five traps

Read before any test

“Ends in -ी, so it is feminine”

Usually true, and that is what makes it dangerous. हाथी, पानी, दही, आदमी, पड़ोसी are masculine, and भाई (brother) is masculine with an -ई ending. So हाथी बड़ा है, not बड़ी. The same goes the other way: कक्षा, परीक्षा, हवा end in -आ and are feminine. When the meaning is a male or female person, trust the person, not the ending.

Putting the plural on the adjective

बड़ियाँ लड़कियाँ looks thorough and is wrong. The noun takes the plural (लड़कियाँ); the adjective only picks a gender form. Feminine, one or many, the adjective is बड़ी both times: बड़ी लड़कियाँ. The plural is announced once.

Changing an adjective that never changes

लाल फूल, लाल किताबें, लाल कुर्सी — लाल is the same three times. So are सुंदर, तेज़, गरम, भारी, गुलाबी. The test is mechanical: does the masculine one-form end in -ा? If not, leave it alone. The instinct to make it match is the instinct you just learned from बड़ा, over-applied.

The wrong word that spells correctly

दीन, पूल, मेल, सुना, जरा, सास — every one is a real word, so nothing flags it. The mistake is only visible against the meaning you intended. When you check your writing, do not ask “is this a word?”. Ask “is this the word I meant?”.

Dots in the wrong place, or too many

The chandrabindu and the nukta each change what a word means or how it sounds, and both are punished if they are missing: कहा (said) is not कहाँ (where); पेड is not पेड़. The reverse also loses marks: native words take no dot, so फूल is right and फ़ूल is not. Dot it when the sound is z, or the flap in ड़ ढ़ — not because a letter looks lonely.
