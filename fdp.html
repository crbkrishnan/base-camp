Three Disguises

A fraction, a decimal and a percentage are not three topics. They are three ways of writing the same thing — and almost every mark in this unit comes from choosing the disguise that makes the arithmetic easiest. Learn to switch, and the hard questions stop being hard.

Five traps

Read before any test

Adding the tops and adding the bottoms

1/2 + 1/3 is not 2/5. Two halves of a pizza is a whole pizza, and 1/2 + 1/3 must be bigger than 1/2 — but 2/5 is smaller than 1/2, so the answer is wrong before you check anything. Common denominator first, every time.

Comparing decimals by counting digits

0.9 is bigger than 0.15, even though 15 looks bigger than 9. Line up the decimal points and pad with zeros: 0.90 against 0.15. Longer is not larger.

Percentage change measured against the wrong number

Percentage change is always (change ÷ ORIGINAL) × 100. Not the new value, not the bigger one — the value you started from. Swap them and you get a plausible-looking wrong answer, which is the worst kind.

Adding a percentage back on to undo it

Take 20% off 100 and you get 80. Put 20% back on 80 and you get 96, not 100. To undo a percentage change you divide by the multiplier — you never subtract the same percentage.

Losing the unit or the sign

"Decreased by 16%" and "16%" are not the same answer. If the question asks for a percentage change, say whether it is an increase or a decrease. That word is frequently the second mark.
