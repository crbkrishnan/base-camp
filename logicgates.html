Switches That Decide

Two ideas run through this whole page. First: a logic gate is fully described by its truth table, so there is nothing to guess, only rows to fill in. Second: a circuit is gates passed along from left to right, so you can always solve it one gate at a time. People lose marks here by trying to do it all in their head.

Five traps

Read before any test

"OR gives 0 when both inputs are 1, because OR means one or the other."

Wrong. In a truth table OR gives 1 when at least one input is 1, and both being 1 counts. The only 0 in an OR column is the 0 0 row. "One or the other but not both" is XOR, which is not examined here.

"A NOT gate flips its two inputs."

A NOT gate has exactly one input and one output. If you see two inputs and a small circle, you are looking at NAND or NOR. The circle on its own means "flip"; it is not a gate.

"NAND is just AND with a circle, so it gives 1 when both inputs are 1."

The circle flips the output of the AND. AND gives 1 only on 1 1, so NAND gives 0 only on 1 1 and 1 on the other three rows. Its table is AND's with every output reversed.

"I wrote the rows in whatever order I thought of them."

You will skip one or write one twice, and you will not notice. Count in binary: 00, 01, 10, 11, and for three inputs 000, 001, 010, 011, 100, 101, 110, 111. Then check the count is 2n.

"NOT A AND B means NOT (A AND B)."

NOT applies only to the thing straight after it, so NOT A AND B means (NOT A) AND B. With A = 1 and B = 0, (NOT A) AND B gives 0 but NOT (A AND B) gives 1. If you mean the whole AND flipped, write the brackets.
