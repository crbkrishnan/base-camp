# Voice

One reader: a Grade 7 student who is capable and knows it, scores around 10 out of 15, and will take any excuse to read instead of work. Write to him, not to a class.

## The register

Direct, unpatronising, occasionally dry. Never cheerful. Never "Let's explore the exciting world of…". Assume he is clever and assume he is avoiding effort — both are true, and the writing should acknowledge the second without nagging about it.

Lines that hit the target, from pages already written:

> Marks only count if you write your answer *before* opening the mark scheme — otherwise you're just reading, and reading feels like learning without being learning.

> Do the job, or the video was a screensaver with maths on it.

> A hint costs you nothing; reading the answer costs you the question.

> If you are not going to write the three lines underneath, close the video and go straight to the questions instead. That is a legitimate choice.

That last one matters: give him real permission to skip things, and the things he does do get done properly.

## Rules

**Name the trap, don't just teach the method.** Anticipate the wrong answer and say it out loud: *"The instinct is to answer 8 m/s, because 4 and 8 average to 6. That instinct is wrong."* He learns more from seeing his own error predicted than from a correct worked example.

**Every number in prose should be checkable.** "Nearly seven times as much energy" is better than "much more energy", and it is true (2260 ÷ 334 = 6.8).

**Explanations end at the idea, not the summary.** No "In conclusion" paragraphs. When the point has landed, stop.

**Cliffhangers between lessons.** Each lesson ends with a question the next one answers: *"Water boils at 100 °C. So how does a puddle disappear on a day that never gets above 15 °C?"*

**Actions, not encouragement.** Instead of "you can do it!", give something to do away from the screen: *"Put one drop of water on the back of each hand. Blow gently on one."*

**Say what the exam actually rewards.** "The word 'perpendicular' is doing real work in that sentence. Leave it out and you can lose the mark." This is tutoring, not textbook writing, and specificity about mark schemes is most of the value.

**Be honest about difficulty.** Label the hard questions hard. Say which two separate a 21 from a 26. Fake reassurance is the fastest way to lose a smart student's trust.

## Targets and framing

Every page states the same deal: he has been scoring 10/15; the page is out of 30; the honest targets are **21 on the first pass and 26 on the second**. Those two numbers sit exactly on the "Strong" and "Top band" rungs of the hub's climb ladder, so the whole library points at one scale. Do not invent different targets per page.

## Formatting

- Body text is serif, UI and headings sans, numbers and data monospace. This is consistent across every page and every PDF.
- Bold for the thing that earns the mark. Italic for asides and tips. Not both.
- Tables when comparing three or more things across the same criteria; prose otherwise.
- Second person throughout. "You", never "the student" or "one".
- Indian rupees and Indian names in worded problems, matching the student's context.
- British spelling and IGCSE terminology: anticlockwise, metre, colour, practise (verb).
