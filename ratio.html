Shares,
Not Amounts

A ratio does not tell you how much anybody gets. It tells you how many parts each person gets, and the parts only become amounts once you know the total. Nearly every mark lost in this topic comes from forgetting that one sentence — usually by reading 3:5 as three-fifths.

The deal. You've been landing around 10 out of 15. This page is out of 30, so the honest target is 21 on the first pass and 26 on the second. The share bar

Set a total, set a ratio, and watch the bar cut itself into equal blocks. Every block is the same size — that is what "one part" means. The shares are just groupings of blocks, which is why you divide by the sum of the numbers in the ratio and never by how many people there are.

Five traps

Read before any test

Reading 3:5 as three-fifths

This is the big one, and it costs more marks than the other four combined. 3:5 means three parts and five parts, so there are eight parts altogether and the first share is 3/8. The colon is not a fraction bar and it is not a division sign. Whenever you see a ratio, add the numbers up first and write the total down.

Dividing by the number of people

"Share 840 between Asha and Ben in the ratio 3:5" — and the pen writes 840 ÷ 2. You divide by the sum of the parts, which is 8, not by how many names appear in the question. The giveaway is that 420 and 420 are equal, and a 3:5 split is obviously not equal.

Simplifying before the units match

40 cm : 1 m is not 40:1. Convert to a common unit first — 40 cm : 100 cm — and then simplify to 2:5. A ratio compares two quantities measured in the same thing, so mixed units must go before anything else happens.

Chaining two ratios by writing them side by side

If A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5, then A:B:C is not 2:3:5. B is three parts in one ratio and four in the other, so the two do not line up. Scale both until B is the same number in each — 12 works — giving 8:12:15.

Treating inverse proportion as direct

Six workers take ten days, so four workers take… and the instinct multiplies by 4/6 to get 6.67 days. Fewer workers cannot be faster. When more of one thing means less of the other, the two multiply to a constant: 6 × 10 = 60 worker-days, so 4 workers take 60 ÷ 4 = 15 days. Always check the direction of your answer before you write it down.
