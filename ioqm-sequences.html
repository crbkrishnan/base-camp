The Gauss staircase

An arithmetic sequence drawn as a staircase of bars. Its upside-down copy (faint) fits on top exactly, and together they make a rectangle n bars wide and (first + last) tall. Half the rectangle is your sum — that's the whole formula.

The fence machine

Two sides of a rectangle, x and y, with a fixed total x + y. Slide x and watch the area x × y rise and fall. The top of the curve is always at x = y — which is the arithmetic mean–geometric mean inequality, drawn.

Five traps

Read before any test

Counting the terms wrongly

From 5 to 101 in steps of 3 is not 96 ÷ 3 = 32 terms. It's (last − first) ÷ step + 1 = 33. The +1 is the fence-post: 10 metres of fence with a post every metre needs 11 posts. Check with something tiny — 5, 8, 11 is (11 − 5) ÷ 3 + 1 = 3 terms.

Using n = 100 for the even numbers up to 100

2 + 4 + … + 100 has 50 terms, not 100. Sum = 50 × (2 + 100) ÷ 2 = 2550. A quicker way: it's 2 × (1 + 2 + … + 50) = 2 × 1275.

Losing track of what survives a telescope

In (1 − ½) + (½ − ⅓) + … + (1/99 − 1/100), everything cancels except the very first and the very last. Write out the first three brackets and the last one before you cancel. Most errors are an off-by-one at the end: 1 − 1/100, not 1 − 1/99.

Using AM–GM on numbers that might be negative

x + y ≥ 2√(xy) is only true for positive x and y. x + 1/x ≥ 2 fails at x = −1, where it's −2. And the inequality is only useful if equality can actually happen: say where it does (x = y) and check that value is allowed.

Dividing by something that could be zero

x² = 5x, divide by x, get x = 5 — and you've lost x = 0. Instead write x² − 5x = 0, so x(x − 5) = 0, so x = 0 or 5. Never cancel an unknown; factorise it out.
