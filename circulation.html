Two Loops, One Pump

Two ideas carry this whole page. First: you do not have one circulation, you have two — heart to lungs and back, heart to body and back — and every drop goes through the heart twice per lap. Second: every vessel is the shape the pressure in it forces it to be. Almost everything else is detail hanging off those two.

Five traps

Read before any test

“Deoxygenated blood is blue”

It is dark red. Oxygenated blood is bright red, deoxygenated blood is a darker red, and neither is ever blue. Diagrams colour it blue so you can follow the two circuits — including the diagram at the top of this page. Write “blue blood” in an exam and you have written something biologically false.

“Arteries carry oxygenated blood, veins carry deoxygenated”

Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry it towards the heart. That is the definition, and it is about direction. The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs and the pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood back — they are not exceptions, they are the reason the rule is worded that way.

Sending blood from the lungs straight to the body

It comes back to the heart first, every time. Lungs → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → body. Skipping the second pass through the heart is skipping the whole point of a double circulation, and it turns a full-mark route description into a zero.

“The left ventricle is thicker because it holds more blood”

Both ventricles hold and eject the same volume — they must, or blood would pile up on one side within a minute. The left wall is thicker because it has to generate a higher pressure to push blood through the far longer, higher-resistance systemic circuit. Distance and resistance, never volume.

“Capillaries are narrow, so blood rushes through”

Blood is slowest in the capillaries — around a thousand times slower than in the aorta. One capillary is narrow, but billions of them side by side have a far greater total cross-sectional area than the aorta, and the same volume passes every point each second. Slow is the point: diffusion needs time.
