Build the Sentence

वाक्य रचना

You read English like a native and write Hindi like a translator. The gap is not vocabulary. Hindi builds a sentence in a different order, glues small words onto the back of nouns, and makes the verb copy things English never asks about. Four rules, a hundred verbs and twenty-four small markers — that is the whole trick, and it is the same trick in a sentence, a paragraph and an essay.

The deal. Last topic you banked 10 out of 15. This page is out of 30, and the honest target is 21 on the first pass and 26 on the second. Marks only count if you write your answer before opening the mark scheme — otherwise you're just reading, and reading feels like learning without being learning. Type your Hindi or write it in a notebook, but write it: nobody has ever got better at sentence construction by nodding at one.

The hundred verbs

100 of 100

Each card shows the verb and three forms you will need every time you write: every day (वह ___ता है), done (past) and will do (future). The badge is the one that matters for Lesson 4: T takes an object and uses ने in the past; I has no object and never does; B can be either. Do not try to learn a hundred in one sitting — ten a day, covered, is ten days.

The marker machine

Pick a noun, choose one or many, and stick a marker on the back of it. Watch the noun. The single most common mistake in Hindi sentences is writing लड़का को — and the machine shows you, live, why it becomes लड़के को.

1 · choose a noun
2 · one or many
3 · stick on a marker
noun + marker The noun changes before the marker arrives. Only masculine -ा nouns change shape in the singular; every noun changes in the plural.

Five traps

Read before any test

Translating word by word

मैं जाता हूँ स्कूल को is English wearing a Hindi costume. The instinct is to keep the English order and swap in Hindi words, because that is how the sentence arrived in your head. Translate in three chunks instead — who | the rest | verb — and say them in that order. The verb is the last thing you write, unless the sentence carries a second verb or a question word to finish.

“लड़का को”

The marker arrives and the noun forgets to change. A masculine -ा word becomes -े in front of any marker (लड़के को, कमरे में, घोड़े से) and every plural takes -ों (लड़कों को, किताबों में). It is the easiest mark on the page to lose, because it looks almost right.

का, की, के copy the wrong noun

सीता का भाई is right, and the instinct says की because Sita is a girl. The marker copies the thing that is owned — भाई, masculine — and pays no attention to the owner. Ask “whose?” first, then look only at the second noun.

The verb copies the doer after ने

राम ने रोटी खाया. Once ने appears, the verb stops copying the doer and copies the object (रोटी, feminine → खाई). If the object carries को, the verb gives up on both and stays in the plain masculine singular (राम ने सीता को बुलाया).

One English word, one Hindi word

It does not work that way, in either direction. से is doing four English jobs (from, by, with-a-tool, than), with is two Hindi words (से for a tool, के साथ for company), and to is को for a person but nothing at all for a place. Ask what job the word is doing before you reach for its usual translation.
