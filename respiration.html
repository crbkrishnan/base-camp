Breathing In, Breathing Out

Two ideas run through this whole page. First: breathing is a pump moving air; respiration is a chemical reaction inside cells. Second: gases only move by diffusion, so lungs are built to make diffusion fast. Everything else is detail hanging off those two.

Four traps

Read before any test

Saying "breathing" when you mean "respiration"

Breathing (ventilation) is the muscular pump that moves air in and out of the lungs. Respiration is the chemical reaction in every cell that releases energy from glucose. Write "respiration happens in the lungs" and you lose the mark, every single time.

Making muscles do the work of pressure

Muscles never push air out or suck air in directly. Muscles change the volume of the thorax; the volume change causes a pressure change; air then moves from high to low pressure. Examiners want that chain in that order.

Thinking oxygen "turns into" carbon dioxide

It doesn't. Glucose is broken down, its carbon ends up in carbon dioxide and its hydrogen in water. Oxygen is a reactant, not a raw material that gets converted.

Mixing cm³ and dm³ halfway through

1000 cm³ = 1 dm³. Tidal volumes are usually quoted in cm³ and ventilation rates in dm³ per minute. Convert once, at the start, and write the unit on every line.
