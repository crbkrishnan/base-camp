Essay Writing

निबंध

A Hindi essay looks like a big job and is really a small one repeated four times. Every essay at this level is a title and four paragraphs, each paragraph carries one idea, and the paragraphs are stitched together by about a dozen linking phrases you can learn in an evening. Add one मुहावरा in the right place and the examiner sees a writer, not a translator. This page gives you the skeleton, the words for the six topics that come up again and again, and two machines: one that tests where a sentence belongs, one that tests whether an upgrade is grammatical.

The deal. Last topic you banked 10 out of 15. This page is out of 30, and the honest target is 21 on the first pass and 26 on the second. Marks only count if you write your answer before opening the mark scheme — otherwise you're just reading, and reading feels like learning without being learning. Write the Hindi. A page you only nodded at is a page you still cannot write.

The sentence upgrader

Same plain sentence, four upgrades: a linker at the front, a describing word before the noun, a मुहावरा in place of a plain phrase, and a reason clause at the back. Some options in every row are wrong on purpose — wrong gender, a linker that points the wrong way, an idiom that means something else. Pick one from each row and the machine says, choice by choice, why it works or why it breaks.

Five traps

Read before any test

The paragraph that wanders

Three sentences about the school building, then one about the principal, then the cricket team. Each sentence is fine and the paragraph is still wrong, because the examiner's tick is for one idea per paragraph. The instinct is to write down everything you know as it arrives. Decide the two ideas before the first sentence, put each in its own paragraph, and if a sentence fits neither, delete it — even a good one.

“बस ख़राब थी, क्योंकि मैं देर से पहुँची”

The linker is right and the sentence is backwards. क्योंकि points at the reason: what comes after it is the cause. इसलिए points at the result. Before you write either, say it in English with ‘because’: I was late because the bus broke down — the bus is the cause, so it goes after क्योंकि.

English order in the middle of a good essay

मैं जाती हूँ स्कूल बस से हर रोज़ sits comfortably in a paragraph of correct sentences, and it costs a mark every time. Long sentences make you slip back into English order without noticing. The check is mechanical: draw a circle round the verb block in every sentence. If a circle is not the last thing in its sentence, fix it. (Exceptions: a closing न or a question word.)

The girl who writes like a boy

मैं रोज़ स्कूल जाता हूँ। मैं बहुत खुश हुआ। The essay is yours, so every ‘I’ verb, adjective and ending must be feminine: जाती हूँ, खुश हुई, चाहती हूँ, बनूँगी. This is the easiest mistake to make because you have heard the masculine form far more often from textbooks and the news. Underline every मैं and check the ending. (For general statements about ‘we’ or ‘students’ use the plain masculine plural, which is correct there.)

The मुहावरा that changed its words

नौ दो बारह हो गए, दाँतों में उँगली दबाई. An idiom is a fixed block: you may bend the verb (tense, gender), never the other words, and never swap one for a ‘similar’ word. The second trap is the wrong feeling: आग-बबूला होना is fury, not joy. If you cannot say what an idiom means in English in five seconds, do not use it. A plain correct sentence beats a fancy wrong one.
