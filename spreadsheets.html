Let the Sheet Do the Work

Two ideas run through this page. First: a spreadsheet does exactly what the formula says and nothing it meant, so a wrong formula gives a confident wrong number with no warning. Second: the data is usually messier than the formula. Everything below is Google Sheets, with its menus and its commas.

The problem lab

26 extra · no marks

These do not count towards the 30. They are here because you are capable of more than 10 out of 15 and some of these are properly hard. Four datasets below: open one, press Copy for Google Sheets, click cell A1 of a blank sheet and paste. Then answer in one of four ways: type a number you got from your own sheet, type a formula and the page runs it, pick an option, or hunt a bug.

How the lab behaves: Check gives feedback that says what you did, not just that it is wrong. A hint unlocks the next hint. The solution unlocks once you have taken a hint or made an attempt — not before. Formulas are tested on the data as it stands, filled down every row where that is part of the task, and on a copy where the numbers have been changed, so a typed-in answer will not pass.
